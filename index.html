About the Project

Background and motivation

Math fact fluency, the ability to recall facts such as multiplication and division quickly and accurately, is a building block for the multi-digit arithmetic, fractions, and measurement that 4th graders take on. Building that fluency takes regular practice, but traditional drills like flash cards and worksheets can feel repetitive, and students often lose interest before the facts become automatic. At the same time, more and more classwork happens on computers, so typing is becoming an everyday skill that young students rarely get a chance to practice on purpose. qw3rty brings these two needs together: it turns math practice into a timed typing race, so students build arithmetic recall and keyboard skills in the same activity, and have a reason to play again to beat their best time.

Who it's for

qw3rty is designed for 4th grade students in Massachusetts, along with the teachers who want an engaging way to reinforce math skills in class or at home. Its problems follow the grade 4 standards in the 2017 Massachusetts Curriculum Framework for Mathematics.

What the math covers

Prompts come from three areas of the grade 4 standards: addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division with whole numbers up to 1,000,000; equivalent fractions and adding and subtracting fractions with denominators of 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, and 100; and converting between imperial units such as feet, inches, and yards. Every problem has a whole-number or fraction answer that is never negative, and division problems always divide evenly.

How it works

A race is 10 math prompts long, and the timer starts as soon as the first prompt appears. Each prompt is shown in light gray with a question mark in place of the answer, for example 10 + 10 = ?. The player types the prompt exactly as shown, then types their answer, entering fractions as a/b, and presses Enter to submit.

Mistakes are shown right away. A mistyped character turns red and is underlined, and the player must fix it with Backspace before they can keep typing. A wrong answer is also marked in red, and the player edits it and submits again. The timer keeps running while they make corrections, so accuracy matters as much as speed. A progress indicator and the elapsed time stay on screen for the whole race.

After the 10th correct answer, the results screen shows the player's total time, how many typing mistakes they made, and how many wrong answers they submitted. The five best times are shown on the main menu and results screen, so students can keep trying to beat their record.

qw3rty runs in a web browser, is built with Vite and React, and can be played entirely with a keyboard. There are no accounts to create and no personal information to enter, and the game uses no analytics, cookies, or third-party scripts.
